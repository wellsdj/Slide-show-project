Chapter 01
What Is
House?

House is an electronic dance genre forged in Chicago's underground clubs. It fuses the soul of disco, the rhythm of funk, and the precision of synthesizers.

Its name comes from the Warehouse — the South Side club where Frankie Knuckles first built the sound that changed dance music forever.

Chapter 02
The Building Blocks

House music is deceptively simple. Four elements stacked on a relentless grid.

●
Kick Drum
Hits on every beat — 1, 2, 3, 4. The four-on-the-floor foundation. Sampled from the Roland TR-909 or TR-808.
◆
Bass Line
Synth bass or TB-303 acid line. Locks in with the kick and provides the groove architecture the body craves.
▲
Lead / Melody
Piano stabs, Juno pads, plucked arps. The emotional layer — the voice that makes a track memorable.
✦
The Mix
Hi-hats, claps, vocal chops, effects. The texture that elevates raw elements into a finished, dynamic track.
Chapter 03
The
History

From a single club in Chicago to every dancefloor on earth — house music is one of the most significant cultural exports of the 20th century.

1977
The Warehouse Opens Frankie Knuckles begins his legendary residency, spinning proto-house on Chicago's South Side.
1984
First House Record "On and On" by Jesse Saunders — pressed on wax, sold from crates, a genre is born.
1987
Acid House Explosion The Roland TB-303 rewires the UK rave scene. House goes global overnight.
Now
Still Evolving Tech house, melodic house, afro house — the four-on-the-floor keeps mutating.

THE
KICK

This is a classic house kick at 130 BPM — the kind that sits deep in the chest before your ears even register it. Four-on-the-floor, every beat, no exceptions.

The low-end is wide and round, with a long sub tail that decays slowly through the beat. It doesn't just hit — it hangs in the room. The transient is tight and punchy on top, giving it that satisfying smack, while the body is warm, dark, and full enough to anchor the entire track on its own.

Everything else in the mix — the bass, the lead — ducks and breathes around this kick. It's the foundation. The clock. The heartbeat.

THE
BASS

Two layers locked into a tight 1/16th grid. First: a short, plucked bass note — tight attack, fast decay, snapping on every sixteenth with a dry, percussive thump that weaves between the kick hits. It's restless and rhythmic, never letting the groove settle.

On top of that sits a cut sawtooth wave from a synthesizer — the raw harmonics of a saw, filtered down to let just enough grit through. Together the two form one voice: pluck on the bottom, saw on top, both sidechained hard to the kick so every time that kick lands, the bass ducks down and swells back up. That pumping, breathing motion is the groove — the invisible glue between the kick and the bass.

THE
LEAD

A single electro pluck patch from Serum — bright, snappy attack with a short sustain and a silky tail. On its own it would be a single note. But played as an arpeggio, it becomes a flowing melodic phrase that cascades up and down the scale, hitting notes in rapid rhythmic sequence and turning a simple sound into movement.

Heavy reverb smears the notes into each other, creating a shimmer that floats above the rhythm. Like the bass, it is sidechained to the kick — so every four-on-the-floor hit pushes the lead back, and it blooms back up in the space between beats. That constant breathing, ducking, and swelling is what makes the track feel alive and three-dimensional.
